# R3.5: scalar-per-cone rescaling experiment

## Direct answer to the reviewer

We implemented scalar-per-cone Ruiz--Pock--Chambolle rescaling and compared it
with the existing coordinate-wise diagonal rescaling on all 62 CBF instances
in `../PDCS_fork/benchmark/represent_data`. Each configuration was run twice
with counterbalanced order, a 3,600 s limit per solve, and a `1e-6` KKT target.
The campaign produced all 248 formal records with no runtime errors.

The primary comparison includes all 62 instances. An unsuccessful run is
charged 3,600 s; the two runs of each mode are first combined per instance by
their geometric mean. The paired geometric-mean scalar/diagonal synchronized
wall-time ratio is **0.9029** (95% paired-bootstrap CI **[0.8099, 0.9780]**),
corresponding to a 9.7% aggregate reduction. Scalar wins 40 instances,
diagonal wins 21, and one is tied by the time-limit cap. The median ratio is
0.9913, so the aggregate improvement is concentrated rather than uniform.

Both modes verify 120/124 runs, verify 59 instances in both repetitions, and
verify 61 instances in at least one repetition. On the 118 run pairs verified
by both modes, scalar/diagonal ratios are 0.950 for PDHG iterations, 0.759 for
projection-wrapper time per iteration, 0.907 for solver end-to-end time, and
0.919 for synchronized `optimize!` wall time.

The proposed size hypothesis is **not supported when dimension means total
problem dimension** `n + m`. The all-instance capped wall-time ratios for the
small, medium, and large corpus terciles are 0.981, 0.944, and 0.796,
respectively; projection time per iteration on jointly verified runs follows
the same ordering, 0.865, 0.768, and 0.650. Some small cases benefit, but the
small group as a whole is indistinguishable from parity. Stratification by
maximum single-cone dimension is non-monotone, and its continuous correlation
with the wall-time ratio is only 0.081.

We therefore expose scalar-per-cone rescaling as a user option but keep the
default `false`. It is useful on this corpus in aggregate and particularly on
some hard SOC cases, but neither total problem dimension nor maximum cone
dimension supports a reliable automatic selection threshold.

This campaign compares diagonal and scalar-per-cone rescaling only. The
no-diagonal-rescaling experiment was already completed separately and was not
rerun or mixed into these results.

## Why diagonal rescaling remains the default

There are two distinct reasons to retain diagonal rescaling.

First, rescaling itself should not be removed. In the separately completed
one-at-a-time ablation, disabling diagonal rescaling reduced the verified count
from 61/62 for the full method to 45/62. That result is not pooled with the new
diagonal-versus-scalar timing statistics, but it shows that diagonal
preconditioning is important for convergence robustness.

Second, the new scalar-per-cone mode does not yet justify replacing
coordinate-wise diagonal rescaling as the default:

- Its aggregate all-instance ratio is favorable, but its median ratio is
  0.991. The typical instance is therefore close to parity, while a few large
  improvements materially affect the geometric mean.
- The effect is heterogeneous. For example, scalar rescaling has a ratio of
  0.073 on `varun` and 0.424 on `qssp180`, but 2.111 on `joint_FC_12` and 1.315
  on `gp_dave_1`.
- Hard cases show repetition sensitivity. On `batchs101006m`, scalar rescaling
  reaches the time limit in repetition 1 but verifies in 2,321 s in repetition
  2; both diagonal runs verify.
- Scalarization reduces the degrees of freedom of the diagonal
  preconditioner. Replacing every factor in a cone by its block maximum makes
  projection cheaper, but it can give up coordinate-wise matrix equilibration
  and consequently worsen convergence on some instances.
- Neither total problem dimension nor maximum structured-cone dimension
  reliably predicts the winner. A size-based automatic switch would therefore
  expose users to regressions without a validated selection rule.
- The present evidence consists of one 62-instance corpus and two repetitions.
  It supports offering the optimization, but is not broad enough to change the
  behavior of all existing workloads by default.

The resulting policy is deliberate: preserve coordinate-wise diagonal
rescaling as the backward-compatible default and let users opt into
scalar-per-cone rescaling when it is advantageous for their workload. This
does not reject the measured 9.7% aggregate gain; it avoids imposing the
observed tail regressions and convergence variability on every problem.

## Method and user option

Both configurations compute the same coordinate-wise candidate factors. For
every SOC, rotated SOC, exponential, or dual-exponential block `B`, the scalar
variant applies

\[
s_B=\max_{j\in B}s_j,
\qquad s_j\leftarrow s_B \quad (j\in B).
\]

This is applied before `scale_data!` in every Ruiz round and in the
Pock--Chambolle pass. One CUDA block reduces one cone. The cumulative factors
remain constant inside each structured cone, allowing constant-scale blocks to
use the ordinary cone-projection paths.

JuMP/MOI users can select the mode explicitly:

```julia
model = Model(PDCS_GPU.Optimizer)
set_optimizer_attribute(model, "rescaling_method", :ruiz_pock_chambolle)
set_optimizer_attribute(model, "scalar_cone_rescaling", true)
```

The following method alias is equivalent:

```julia
set_optimizer_attribute(
    model,
    "rescaling_method",
    :ruiz_pock_chambolle_scalar_cone,
)
```

The direct GPU API accepts the same Boolean keyword. The default remains
`scalar_cone_rescaling=false`. The experiment runner defaults to 3,600 s per
solve and lets users override it with `--time-limit SECONDS`.

The focused GPU tests pass 13/13 assertions for the public option,
scalarization kernel, empty range, and constant-scale projection dispatch.
The Boolean and method-alias solve entry points additionally pass 3/3 tests.

| Jointly verified synchronized wall ratio | 0.9304 | 0.9088 |
| Jointly verified projection/iteration ratio | 0.7500 | 0.7670 |

The projection-cost effect is stable, but convergence outcomes on the hardest
cases vary between repetitions. In repetition 1, `batch` and `varun` are
verified only by scalar, while `batchs101006m` is verified only by diagonal.
In repetition 2, `joint_FC_12` is verified only by diagonal. The
`integrated_KleinesBeispiel_02_6` instance reaches the time limit in both modes
and both repetitions. This is why neither a single steady-state repetition nor
the jointly solved subset alone is used as the primary result.

## Size analysis

The tercile cutoffs are determined once from all 62 input files. Ties at a
cutoff can make group counts unequal. Ratios below one favor scalar rescaling.

### Total problem dimension `n + m`

| Group | Range | Instances | All-instance capped wall ratio | 95% CI | Median | Scalar/diagonal/tie wins | Projection/iteration ratio on verified pairs |
|:---|:---|---:|---:|:---|---:|:---|---:|
| Small | <= 378 | 21 | 0.9810 | [0.9439, 1.0117] | 1.0013 | 10 / 11 / 0 | 0.8647 |
| Medium | 379--2,777 | 20 | 0.9445 | [0.8612, 1.0237] | 0.9932 | 12 / 8 / 0 | 0.7680 |
| Large | > 2,777 | 21 | 0.7962 | [0.5966, 0.9809] | 0.9248 | 18 / 2 / 1 | 0.6505 |

The log-size/log-ratio correlation is -0.121. It is weak, but its direction
and the tercile table both contradict the proposed diminishing benefit with
larger total dimension. The small group is effectively at parity.

### Maximum structured-cone dimension

| Group | Range | Instances | All-instance capped wall ratio | 95% CI | Median | Scalar/diagonal/tie wins | Projection/iteration ratio on verified pairs |
|:---|:---|---:|---:|:---|---:|:---|---:|
| Small | <= 3 | 33 | 0.9355 | [0.8829, 0.9820] | 0.9827 | 24 / 9 / 0 | 0.7372 |
| Medium | 4--12 | 8 | 0.8277 | [0.6585, 1.0019] | 0.9288 | 4 / 4 / 0 | 0.9801 |
| Large | > 12 | 21 | 0.8828 | [0.6602, 1.0730] | 0.9937 | 12 / 8 / 1 | 0.7196 |

The continuous log-size/log-ratio correlation is 0.081, weakly in the
direction of diminishing benefit, but the group means are non-monotone and the
medium and large confidence intervals include one. These data do not justify
a maximum-cone-dimension threshold either.

## Cone-family breakdown

| Cone family | Instances | All-instance capped wall ratio | 95% CI | Projection/iteration ratio on verified pairs |
|:---|---:|---:|:---|---:|
| EXP only | 5 | 0.9872 | [0.9667, 1.0033] | 0.9540 |
| SOC only | 40 | 0.9388 | [0.8765, 1.0047] | 0.6591 |
| SOC + EXP | 17 | 0.8024 | [0.5672, 1.0006] | 0.9991 |

The direct projection mechanism is clearest for SOC-only problems. The mixed

geometric mean is influenced by a few large gains.

## Heterogeneity and correctness

Representative two-repetition capped wall ratios illustrate the spread:

| Instance | `n + m` | Maximum cone dimension | Scalar/diagonal ratio |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| `varun` | 3,024 | 28 | 0.073 |
| `qssp180` | 718,209 | 4 | 0.424 |
| `batch` | 325 | 6 | 0.736 |
| `batchs101006m` | 2,086 | 21 | 1.181 |
| `gp_dave_1` | 1,960 | 3 | 1.315 |
| `joint_FC_12` | 1,040,357 | 105,589 | 2.111 |

In repetition 2, the 60 jointly verified solutions have a median relative
primal-objective difference of `3.22e-9` and a maximum of `7.79e-5`; both
solutions in every reported pair independently pass the KKT target. The
largest verified KKT maximum is `9.98e-7` for scalar and `9.97e-7` for
diagonal.

`projection_time_sec` is the solver's existing host-side accumulator around
projection wrappers. It is useful as a paired diagnostic, but it is not a
CUDA-event trace and should not be interpreted as an exact sum of device
kernel durations. No profiling kernels were used in the timed campaign. The
reviewer-facing performance claim is therefore based on synchronized
`optimize!` wall time; the projection ratios explain the mechanism.
